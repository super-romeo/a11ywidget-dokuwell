How it works

Many visitors need a website to adapt to them: larger text, higher contrast, more space between lines, a font that is easier to read. Most sites do not offer this, and visitors who need it leave or struggle through.

At the same time, most sites have accessibility issues in their code: images without alternative text, buttons that only show an icon, form fields without labels. These are best fixed in the site itself, but that takes developer time, and new issues appear with every update.

The widget works on both sides. You add one line of code to your site or install it through your platform's plugin. A small button appears in the corner of every page.

Visitors who press it can change how the page is shown: text size, contrast, line and letter spacing, a readable font, a stop to animations, a highlighted focus for keyboard users. Their choice is remembered on the next visit.

On its own side, the widget patches common issues in the page as it loads: it adds missing labels to form fields where the label is clear from the context, marks decorative images, and adds missing page language. These patches help while the site itself is being fixed.

Every day the site is scanned. The daily report lists the issues the widget cannot fix by itself, page by page and grouped by template: images that need a real description, contrast that needs a design change, structure that needs a developer. Each issue comes with a short explanation of how it is usually fixed.

The report also shows what changed since the last scan, so a theme update or a new plugin that adds issues is noticed the next day.

A draft accessibility statement is generated from the scan results. It describes honestly what the site offers, what was fixed and what is still being worked on, and you can edit it before publishing.

A widget does not make a website fully compliant with WCAG on its own, and we do not claim that it does. Real accessibility comes from fixing the site itself; the widget gives visitors options now and gives you a clear, daily list of what to fix.

Agencies that look after several client sites can add each site separately, see every daily report in one list and send each client a short monthly summary of what was fixed and what remains on their site.

The service is built for owners of websites and online shops anywhere. It is in preparation: we are checking whether site owners need it before it is built.
